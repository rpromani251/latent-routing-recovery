Experiment B: the full-pipeline bootstrap is both wrong and unnecessary

Closes the sec.17 open item ("no full-pipeline bootstrap yet") and the Stage-3
calibration item in sec.10 of boundary_recovery_v5. Run at B = 300 as registered,
against a B = 20,000 reference null. Both halves of the registered recipe fail, in
opposite directions, and four further results came out of the same harness.

THE RECIPE DOES NOT PRODUCE A NULL. Regenerating responses from resampled INLIER
residuals inflates the null LRT median from a true 0.65 to 67.79 (q95 91.65), so every
anchor returns p = 1.000 on honest and gated surfaces alike. The mechanism is shape,
not scale: the inlier set is the truncated middle 75% of the residuals (kurtosis 2.11
against the noise's 2.99), and feeding it back through a pipeline that trims again
compounds the deformation, at which point an equal-variance two-component mixture fits
a flat-topped sample far better than one Gaussian does. Resampling ALL residuals fails
differently -- under a gate it resamples the gate's own bimodality into the null
(median 11.91 at Delta/tau = 1.95 against 1.08 on honest), also firing 0.000. Only
Gaussian regeneration works, and the scale of the regenerated noise is provably
irrelevant (LTS is scale-equivariant, both tests scale-invariant).

AND THE PRACTICE IT WAS MEANT TO REPLACE WAS NEVER MISCALIBRATED. The stated premise
is that "LTS truncates the tails before the LRT sees them". It does not: Stage 2
residualises ALL points against the refit plane, so the trim selects the FIT, not the
TEST SAMPLE. At B = 20,000 each:
    pipeline null        median 0.657   q95 5.459   q99 8.622
    clean, untrimmed     median 0.702   q95 5.543   q99 8.905
Using the clean q95 on the pipeline's own null gives size 0.0476 rather than 0.050.
The July figure of 6.08 is reproduced exactly by clean draws at m = 400, so that gap
looks like a sample-size difference, not a trimming effect -- suggestive, since the
July m was not recorded. The null does drift weakly with m; calibrate at the deployed m.

THE NULL IS NOT PER-ANCHOR OR PER-RUNG. Between-rung sd of q95 is 0.049 against a
within-rung replicate sd of 0.123, and it is invariant to a 10x increase and 100x
decrease in tau, to a 100x trend and to no trend at all -- as it must be, since the
residual is invariant to anything in the span of [1, Z]. The observed per-anchor spread
of the B = 300 threshold is 0.51 against 0.50 for pure Monte-Carlo resampling of the
reference: ALL of it is noise, there is no anchor structure to condition on. So task
9's 5.50 / 5.35 / 4.60 is one threshold measured three times, and the spread is not
free -- the deepest-rung 4.60 inflates size to 0.077. One calibration computed once at
high B is 20x more accurate than B = 300 and 300x cheaper. B = 300's own q95 has a
2.5-97.5 range of 4.47 to 6.51 around a true 5.46.

Level yes, p-value no: the level is exact (0.050 over 300 honest anchor-rungs) and
p-values are uniform below ~0.8, but 16.7% pile up at 1.000 because the equal-variance
LRT has an atom at zero under H0 (19.9% of honest anchors, matching the null's 19.9%).
Usable as tests at a fixed level, not as evidence measures.

THE MINIMUM-MASS RULE IS AN ANTI-GATE. Carried from 125555a and now measured. On a
surface with NO gate anywhere it declares the rung estimable at 0.990 / 0.995 / 0.990;
at gated rungs the ball never reaches (d/sigma = 5.13, true pi = 0) it passes at 0.995
-0.998. It is not a gate, it is very nearly a constant. Worse, it is anti-correlated
with the truth: at Delta/tau = 5, d/sigma = 2.56 -- about four real crossers in 800 --
it passes only 0.748, because EM finds the real minority, pi_hat lands below 0.05 and
the rule rejects. The rule fails when EM is right and passes when EM splits noise. The
calibrated test is nominal exactly where min-mass is 0.99, so the deepest-estimable-
rung convention should be defined by the test, and then it does self-enforce.

THE DIP'S FLOOR IS A FUNCTION OF PI. The ~2 sd figure in sec.11 is the EQUAL-WEIGHT
floor. Separation at which the calibrated dip reaches power 0.5, n = 800:
    pi     0.50   0.35   0.25   0.10   0.05
    sep    3      4      4      6      >7
Anchor placement targets pi <= 0.10 (step 2), so at its own operating point the dip is
inert below ~6 sd -- three times the stated floor, against a real scaffold at 1.6-1.95.
That is the structural reason behind 125555a's "the dip contributes nothing"; dip-OR-LRT
== LRT is a property of low pi, not of that separation. Separately, the dip's tabled
p-value fires at 0.000 on honest anchors at every rung; calibrating it through the
pipeline restores nominal size but buys no power (0.033-0.062 on every gated cell) and
raises resonant-curvature FP from 0.058 to 0.557. Leave the dip on its tabled p-value.

DETECTION POWER PEAKS WHERE ORIENTATION DOES, so the pi conflict is the LADDER's, not
orientation's. Power against distance, Delta/tau = 2.5:
    d/sigma   0.39   0.77   1.28   1.54   2.56   5.13
    power     0.158  0.200  0.628  0.720  0.080  0.032
The peak at d/sigma ~ 1.3-1.6 is the same shell step 2 identified as orientation-useful
and the same region the distance estimator prefers. Detection, distance and orientation
all want one rung and it is the same rung. What conflicts is the ladder: anchored with
its top rung at the sweet spot it necessarily puts the lower rungs at d/sigma 2.56 and
5.13, where nothing is estimable and power is at size. At pi_top = 0.35 the ordering
inverts and the DEEPEST rung becomes the good one (0.720 at d/sigma 1.54) while the top
rung sits at 0.158, too close for LTS to have a majority branch. The design variable is
where the sweet spot sits in the ladder, not which pi to prefer -- a ladder climbing
away from the boundary keeps every rung estimable. Not yet run.

Power at the real target for the record: Delta/tau = 1.95 gives 0.203 at d/sigma = 1.28,
and 1.5 gives 0.065 -- far below the 0.42-0.54 sec.11 quotes at 1.5 sd, because that
figure is at pi = 0.25 and placement now targets pi <= 0.10. Pooling is the detector.

MACHINERY. sim/fast_em.py is a batched rewrite of gmm2_equalvar for the null replicates:
the equal-variance responsibility is sigmoid(a + b*y), linear in the observation, so the
(chains x points x components) tensor collapses to a few (chains x points) passes and
sum(y), sum(y^2) are fixed across iterations. Same inits, update order, convergence test
and best-of-inits rule, including the reference's +1e-12 on the component counts.
Verified against the reference over 60 residual shapes spanning LRT -0.02 to 218: max
relative difference 5e-10. Blocked at 250 replicates (B = 6000 costs 140 s unblocked,
40 s blocked; identical at every block size). verify_bootstrap.py runs 10 checks and
exits 0.

Three defects noted, not fixed here: exp_a_invariance.py and exp_p_pooling.py load and
write through hardcoded /tmp paths so neither runs from a fresh checkout;
exp_p_pooling.py:313 has a cancelling +mean-mean that looks like a half-applied centring
correction; and gmm2_equalvar's convergence test is relative to |ll|, which shifts by
-n log k under rescaling, so the LRT is not exactly scale-invariant in implementation
(1.6e-3 over a factor of 2, negligible against a threshold of 5.46).

SCOPE. Intrinsic d = 2, frame supplied, Stage 0 deferred, density filter a no-op -- so
"full pipeline" here is trim -> refit -> residualise -> both tests. The filter branch is
uncalibrated, the separability guard still has no calibrated null (and sec.8 shows
curvature fires the LRT at up to 1.000, making the guard the binding component), and
the parametric calibration assumes Gaussian noise -- the nonparametric alternatives that
would not are exactly the ones ruled out here.
