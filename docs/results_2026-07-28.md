# Stage A results — 28 July 2026

Everything below is against `routing_audit_v2` (24 July). Scalar output, so `V = 1` and most
of Stage A's vector machinery collapses: whitening is division by a scalar, `λ_max(S̃)` is the
whitened response variance, and §3.5's "dip on the first principal component" becomes dipping
the whitened responses directly — no projection choice, no Bonferroni over directions, no
projection-pursuit ambiguity.

---

## 1. Headline

**Dispersion does not discriminate. Modality does.**

| | honest model | gated model |
|---|---|---|
| `R^log` flag rate | 0.048 | 0.069 |
| dip flag rate at σ\* | **0.000** | **0.213** |

Seattle building energy, n = 3,581 buildings, gate = `f = h − 0.30·1[b(x) ≥ 0.25]`.
Zero dip false positives across 3,420 honest-model buildings.

The dispersion curve's *shape* is not identifying, and this is structural rather than a bad
choice of statistic. A hard gate viewed through a probe of scale σ is observationally a
transition of width ≈ σ, and a stationary GP with lengthscale ℓ ≈ σ reproduces the same
dispersion curve. So **no statistic computed from `r_i(σ)` alone can separate them** — not the
range, not a shape statistic, not the studentized version.

What differs is the *distribution* of responses, not their spread. A gate splits probes into
two clumps separated by the penalty; a smooth branch does not. That makes v2 §3.5 the
discriminator and demotes §3.1–3.4 to a device for localizing σ\*.

## 2. Detection radius

Detection is local. Flags trace the gate boundary rather than filling the penalized region.

| true distance to boundary | dip flag rate |
|---|---|
| 0–50 m | 0.978 |
| 50–100 m | 0.783 |
| 100–200 m | 0.661 |
| 200–400 m | 0.745 |
| 400–800 m | 0.480 |
| 800–1600 m | 0.165 |
| far / none | 0.030 |

Flagged buildings are enriched 3.9× for gated tracts (0.301 vs base rate 0.077).

This is the empirical content of §6.3's "unflagged is not interior": the audit can only see a
boundary from within roughly a probe-width of it, so the recoverable estimand is
*boundary-adjacent territory*, not the full partition.

## 3. Conformal conditional coverage

Split conformal at nominal 90% on the gated model, half-width 0.877 log-EUI:

- marginal coverage **0.898**
- coverage | unpenalized branch **0.903**
- coverage | penalized branch **0.840**
- conditional gap **+0.063**

The penalty (0.30) is well below the model's own residual scale, so marginal coverage lands
essentially on nominal. An auditor checking aggregate coverage sees nothing wrong. The failure
is entirely conditional, and the routing partition is exactly the grouping a Mondrian/
group-conditional conformal predictor would need in order to fix it.

## 4. What did not work, and why

**Uncalibrated `R^log` (v2 §3.2 S1).** Against smooth GP nulls drawn from the registered class,
flag rates of 0.65–1.00 at a threshold calibrated to 5% on a linear null. It has no specificity;
it is an unnormalized effect size, not a detector. King County looked clean only because that
honest model is smooth on a lengthscale far longer than the probe ladder — an easy null.

**Shape statistics.** Under the registered null the *expected* dispersion curve is monotone
non-increasing:

```
r(σ)² τ² = ‖β‖² + s²(1 − (1 + 2σ²/ℓ²)^(−d/2)) / σ²
```

with the second term decreasing in σ. But an individual GP *sample path* wiggles, and a wiggle
at one scale is indistinguishable in shape from a crossing. Worst-case null 0.60 vs power 0.76.

**Calibrated `T_scale` (v2 §3.2 S2).** Two failures, in order:
1. Population-level null — hyperparameters redrawn per replicate — inherits all cross-anchor
   spread in (β, s, ℓ). Those per-anchor fits are noisy, the covariance inflates, the null is
   over-dispersed and nothing looks extreme. Power 0.025, worst null 0.327. Note §3.2 specifies
   μ₀, s₀ "at anchor i" — the hierarchy is meant to *regularize* each anchor's fit, not replace it.
2. Anchor-specific null with an `ell_min` floor — specificity recovers (worst null 0.140) but
   power goes to 0.000, because with `ell_min = 0.05` the GP can still fit the gate. Raising
   `ell_min` is the only lever, and it *is* the declaration of what counts as legitimately smooth.

Implementation note not in v2: the minimum-signal rule must be applied **per scale**, not per
anchor. Where σ·|slope| < τ_obs the whitened variance falls below the noise floor, `r` clamps to
zero, and `log(r + ε)` pins to the epsilon floor — first run produced `R^log ≈ 18`, entirely
the clamp.

## 4b. Is the lengthscale condition satisfied? (verified)

Dispersion is only informative when the honest model is straight at every probed scale,
i.e. ℓ > σ_T. Measured two independent ways on the gate-free honest model:

**(A) Parametric**, fitting the closed form to honest dispersion curves (n = 475 anchors):
the fitted ℓ̂ is *unidentified* for most anchors — median ℓ̂ pins to the lower bound —
because the local curvature amplitude is negligible, **s ≈ 0.004 log-EUI**. With no
curvature to constrain it, any ℓ fits equally well. The meaningful number here is s, and
it is **75× smaller than the 0.30 gate penalty**.

**(B) Non-parametric**, spatial correlation of the honest location surface with the linear
trend removed: correlation falls to e^(−1/2) at **2,367 m**, against a ladder top of
1,200 m.

Both say the same thing: over every distance probed, the honest EUI surface is
essentially locally linear. **The condition holds, with roughly a factor of 2 in
lengthscale and a factor of 75 in amplitude to spare.** That is why `R^log` calibrates
sensibly here — and it is exactly the assumption that fails against the synthetic
smooth-GP nulls, where amplitude and lengthscale were deliberately set comparable to the
gate.

Caveat: this is verifiable here only because we *have* the honest model. A real auditor
sees only the deployed model and cannot run this check, which is why the dip — needing no
such assumption — is the more defensible primitive.

## 5. Model class

Boosted trees are piecewise constant and formally outside the smooth null. Empirically the gap is
mild in 10 dimensions — 12% of anchors peaked vs 3% for a spline fit — because an ambient probe
crosses many splits at once and the ensemble averages out. Not disqualifying, but the honest EUI
model here is spline + ridge so the null class holds by construction.

## 6b. On-manifold probes, multiplicity, spatial randomization, K=2 recovery

### On-manifold (kNN) probe family — v2 §2.2

Probe points resampled from **real building locations** with weights ∝ exp(−d²/2σ²), so
support realism is exact by construction. Support is discrete, so scales with fewer than
12 distinct neighbours are abstained from: median valid scales 5/12 on-manifold vs 6/12
ambient. That sets a minimum resolvable scale from data density.

| | ambient | on-manifold |
|---|---|---|
| dip false positives, honest model | 0.0000 | 0.0028 |
| dip flag rate, gated model | 0.214 | 0.157 |
| after BH q=0.10 | 0.197 | 0.139 |
| after BY q=0.10 | 0.165 | 0.118 |

**The finding survives restriction to realistic inputs.** Within 50 m the two probe
families are equivalent (0.933 ambient vs 0.944 on-manifold); on-manifold decays faster at
long range (400–800 m: 0.439 vs 0.266), which is expected since real buildings thin out
near tract edges. The flags are not artifacts of probing Puget Sound.

BY penalty c(n) = 8.68, i.e. BY is BH at q/8.68. Flags survive it.

### Spatial randomization — the alignment claim

Rather than controlling FDR over 3,581 dependent tests, test what we care about: do flags
concentrate near gate boundaries? Permute b **across tracts**, holding tract geometry and
building locations fixed. Valid under arbitrary spatial dependence; no null generator.

| statistic | ambient p | on-manifold p |
|---|---|---|
| frac ≤ 50 m | 0.083 | **0.013** |
| frac ≤ 100 m | 0.198 | **0.025** |
| frac ≤ 200 m | 0.208 | **0.033** |
| median distance | 0.128 | **0.025** |

**Ambient never clears; on-manifold always does.** Consistent across all four statistics,
so not a statistic-choice artifact. Ambient flags carry a real but diffuse signal that the
permutation null absorbs — long-range ambient flags dilute the alignment. This is a second,
independent argument for making on-manifold the primary probe family: it both removes the
support-realism objection and produces the stronger formal claim.

### K=2 regime recovery — v2 §4.1 + B3

At flagged anchors, fit a 2-component Gaussian mixture to a **fresh S3 draw** (budget split
respected: S1 dispersion/σ\*, S2 dip, S3 recovery). Soft responsibilities give π̂ᵢ; the
component-mean gap estimates the penalty; the unperturbed response is posterior-assigned
for anchor orientation. Synchronization is trivial at K=2 — order components by mean.

**Recovered penalty Δ̂ against a planted 0.300:**

| probe | subset | n | median Δ̂ | IQR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ambient | dip-flagged, gated | 704 | **0.299** | [0.292, 0.302] |
| ambient | honest model | 3422 | 0.013 | [0.012, 0.015] |
| on-manifold | dip-flagged, gated | 499 | **0.298** | [0.291, 0.301] |
| on-manifold | honest model | 3162 | 0.013 | [0.012, 0.016] |

Recovery to within 0.002 with an IQR of about ±0.005, and the honest-model baseline of
0.013 confirms the mixture does not manufacture structure where none exists. This is
recovery, not detection: the magnitude of the hidden penalty is estimated from black-box
queries alone.

## 6c. What is the dispersion stage actually for?

Three candidate roles were tested. Two fail, one holds.

### Role 1 — scale selection (FAILS)

Dip at the dispersion-selected σ\* vs. other ways of choosing where to dip (n = 906
on-manifold anchors; power measured on anchors within 400 m of a boundary):

| scale-selection rule | power | FP honest |
|---|---|---|
| σ\* (dispersion peak) | 0.715 | 0.001 |
| a random valid scale | 0.798 | 0.004 |
| fixed 1200 m | 0.907 | 0.009 |
| best fixed, 844 m (hindsight) | 0.966 | 0.002 |

σ\* is **worse than random**. Mechanism: `r(σ)` divides by σ, so it peaks at small σ,
whereas the dip needs a *balanced mixing fraction*, which occurs at larger σ. The two
steps want opposite things. The gap widens with distance — at 200–400 m, σ\* gets 0.591
against 0.966 for the fixed scale.

### Role 2 — anchor screening / budget concentration (FAILS)

`R^log` ranks anchors for dip-flagging at AUC **0.750** — real signal, weak. To retain 90%
of the flags you would get by dipping everything, you must still dip **70%** of anchors.
And the screen costs 12 × 200 = 2,400 queries per anchor to save a 600-query dip at 30% of
anchors: query-negative by roughly 4×. (Parameter-dependent — a cheaper screen could flip
the arithmetic; see the budget curve.)

### Role 3 — corroboration (HOLDS)

2×2 with flag counts matched, on-manifold, truth = within 400 m of a boundary:

| | n | truly near a gate |
|---|---|---|
| neither | 2433 | 0.030 |
| dispersion only | 318 | 0.182 |
| dip only | 318 | 0.516 |
| **both** | 246 | **0.805** |
| base rate | 3315 | 0.149 |

Cohen's κ = 0.32 — the two statistics see genuinely different things, which is why stacking
them helps. Dispersion alone is near-useless (0.182 vs 0.149 base rate). Requiring both
lifts precision 0.516 → 0.805 at the cost of recall (564 → 246 flags).

### Query-budget curve under UNKNOWN gate scale

The black-box setting: the auditor does not know the gate's spatial scale. Naive scanning
means dipping at k log-spaced scales with Bonferroni over the k.

| strategy | queries/anchor | power | FP honest |
|---|---|---|---|
| blind guess, 1 scale | 600 | 0.488 | 0.0022 |
| naive scan, 2 scales | 1,200 | 0.854 | 0.0066 |
| naive scan, 4 scales | 2,400 | **0.929** | 0.0044 |
| dispersion → σ\*, 1 dip | 3,000 | 0.715 | 0.0011 |
| naive scan, 6 scales | 3,600 | 0.962 | 0.0033 |
| dispersion → dip all valid scales | 6,000 | 0.978 | 0.0055 |
| naive scan, 12 scales | 7,200 | 0.974 | 0.0033 |

**Naive scanning dominates at every budget.** Bonferroni over a handful of scales is cheap
— α/4 still leaves a threshold real detections clear easily — while the dispersion curve
costs 2,400 queries before the first dip and then points at the wrong scale.

### Consequence for the algorithm

- **Existence claim** ("does this model route at all?"): drop Stage A.1–A.4. Dip at 4–6
  log-spaced scales, Bonferroni across them, BY across anchors. Cheaper, more powerful, and
  assumption-free about the gate's scale.
- **Structure claim** ("which buildings are penalized?"): keep dispersion as a
  *corroborator*, not a screen or selector. It buys precision 0.52 → 0.81.

Caveat: one gate geometry, one dataset (threshold on tract demographics, tract-scale
boundaries). A gate at a very different scale, or running *along* the data manifold rather
than across it, could reorder this. The toy suite should be re-run under the naive-scan
protocol before the claim generalizes.

## 6d. Seattle re-run under the simplified protocol — HEADLINE NUMBERS

Protocol: on-manifold (kNN) probes · naive scan over 6 log-spaced scales, 25 m–1200 m ·
dip at each · Bonferroni over the scales actually dipped · **no dispersion curve, no σ\***.
K=2 mixture at every scale gives Δ̂ for free. n = 3,581; median 4 of 6 scales dipped.

**Ground truth corrected.** Precision was previously scored against "within 400 m of a
boundary," which is arbitrary. The honest criterion is whether the probe neighbourhood
*actually contains a crossing*: π_true = fraction of probe points on the far side, and an
anchor is detectable when π_true ≥ 0.05. A building 600 m out probed at 800 m does cross,
so flagging it is correct. 1,072 anchors (29.9%) are detectable in this sense.

### Detection

| | |
|---|---|
| flag rate, gated model | 0.228 (816 buildings) |
| flag rate, honest model | **0.0075** |
| power on detectable anchors | **0.737** |
| flag rate where no crossing is reachable | 0.0104 |

### Precision

| rule | flags | precision | recall | honest FP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dip alone | 816 | 0.968 | 0.737 | 0.0075 |
| dip + Δ̂ > 0.05 | 697 | 0.990 | 0.644 | 0.0011 |
| **dip + Δ̂ > 0.15** | 652 | **1.000** | 0.608 | 0.0011 |
| dip + Δ̂ ∈ [0.2, 0.4] | 556 | 1.000 | 0.519 | 0.0011 |
| dip + Δ̂ > 0.15 + CV < 0.15 | 374 | 1.000 | 0.349 | 0.0011 |

**Recommended operating point: dip at 4–6 scales with Bonferroni, keep flags with
Δ̂ > 0.15.** Zero false positives across 652 flags, 61% of what is findable, 0.1% on the
honest model.

### Recovery

| subset | n | median Δ̂ | IQR |
|---|---|---|---|
| flagged, gated | 816 | **0.291** | [0.152, 0.299] |
| unflagged, gated | 2764 | 0.013 | [0.013, 0.015] |
| honest model | 3581 | 0.014 | [0.013, 0.015] |

### Caveat on "corroboration"

Δ̂ and the dip are computed from the same response distribution at the same anchor — a
large recovered jump and a significant dip are two readings of the same bimodality, not
independent evidence. So "dip + Δ̂" is one test plus an effect-size filter (the
statistical- vs practical-significance distinction), not replication. Dispersion was
genuinely more independent (κ = 0.32) but much weaker. The trade is independence against
discriminating power, and Δ̂ wins on the axis that matters here.

## 6e. Query allocation

Power is limited by the **sample size of the minor mode**, m·π_i, not by the choice of
test. At π = 0.075 with m = 500 the minor mode holds ~37 points; the dip is being asked to
see a 37-point shoulder on a 460-point bulk.

On the failing band (π_true 0.05–0.10, n = 534), at **equal query budget**:

| protocol | queries | power | honest FP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 scales × m=500 | 6,000 | 0.472 | 0.0000 |
| **3 scales × m=1000** | 6,000 | **0.875** | 0.0000 |

Above π = 0.10 detection was already perfect, so extra scales were wasted budget.
Projected overall power on detectable anchors: **0.737 → ~0.94**.

Caveat: Δ̂ on weak-signal anchors is 0.158, not 0.30 — a small minor component is
under-resolved, so Δ̂ is biased downward at small π. The Δ̂ > 0.15 precision filter would
cut 143 of these 534. Report the filter as a precision/recall curve, not a fixed threshold.

## 7. Implications for routing_audit_v2

What the experiments say the theory document should change.

### Demote the dispersion apparatus (§3.1–§3.4)

`R^log` / `T_scale` fails in all three roles tested:
- **not a detector** — flags smooth GP nulls at 0.65–1.00
- **not a scale selector** — σ\* is worse than a random scale (0.715 vs 0.798)
- **not an efficient screen** — AUC 0.750, retains 90% of flags only by dipping 70% of
  anchors, and costs ~4× the queries it saves

The surviving role is corroboration (precision 0.52 → 0.81), but the recovered effect size
Δ̂ dominates it there. **Replace Stage A.1–A.4 with a naive multi-scale dip scan.**

### Promote the dip (§3.5) from corroboration to primary

A hard gate viewed at probe scale σ is observationally a smooth transition of width ≈ σ, so
a GP of lengthscale ℓ ≈ σ reproduces its dispersion curve exactly. **No statistic computed
from r(σ) alone can separate them** — this is an identifiability fact, not a bad statistic.
What differs is the *distribution* of responses. Modality identifies; dispersion cannot.

### Drop the null generator (§3.2 S2, §3.3)

The hierarchical GP generator exists solely to calibrate the dispersion statistic. If
dispersion is not primary, the generator goes with it — and with it the `ell_min`
registration problem, the contamination/cross-fitting machinery, and the misspecification
exposure. Large simplification.

### Probe family is decisive, not a stratification (§2.2)

v2 treats ambient vs on-manifold as a reporting caveat. Empirically it determines whether
the spatial claim is significant at all: ambient p = 0.083–0.208, on-manifold p =
0.013–0.033, across every statistic tried. **On-manifold should be the primary family**,
with ambient as the robustness check — the reverse of the current framing.

### The estimand should be the boundary, not the partition (§13 P1)

v2 targets "the restriction of the partition {g⁻¹(k)} to probe-accessible territory." But
detection has a **radius**: you only see a boundary from within roughly a probe-width of
it. So the natural estimand is the boundary itself — the level set where g flips — and
per-anchor classification is a derived quantity. This is both more honest and easier to
attain: a boundary segment is recovered if *any* nearby anchor detects it.

### Query allocation is a first-class design parameter

v2 fixes m implicitly and scans many scales. The evidence says the opposite: fewer scales,
more probes each. Add a design rule — allocate to keep m·π above the dip's power threshold
in the weakest band you care about.

### Multiplicity: spatial randomization over BH/BY (§3.4)

BH assumes independence or PRDS; anchors are strongly dependent. Permuting b across tracts
gives arbitrary-dependence validity *and* more power, because it tests the alignment claim
directly rather than 3,581 per-anchor claims.

### Promote the anchor graph from Stage B to Stage A (§5 SYNC1)

SYNC1 already allows "geographic, manifold, or task-metric" neighborhoods. Pooling evidence
across that graph is the route to recovering weak-signal anchors — and because the graph
need not be geographic, **the method generalizes beyond the geospatial setting**. This is
arguably the largest available claim and it is currently buried in Stage B.

### Split Stage B (§4–§8, §13)

v2 presents Stage B as uniformly hard — synchronization over partial matchings,
certification, a missing theorem. But **K=2 local recovery with a pure anchor needs none of
that**, and it works: Δ̂ = 0.291 against a planted 0.300, honest-model baseline 0.014.
Stage B should be split into *local K=2 recovery* (solved) and *global general-K
synchronization* (open).

## 7b. Attribute-graph experiment — NEGATIVE RESULT, with a diagnosis worth keeping

Test of the generalization claim: same machinery, non-geographic anchor graph (kNN over
standardized building attributes), non-spatial gate (`YearBuilt < 1960` — a vintage gate
that makes pre-1960 stock look more efficient than it is and screens it out of retrofit
programs). **It does not work on this dataset**, and the reason is a genuine precondition
nobody had stated.

### What went wrong, in three passes

1. **Graph too coarse.** Attributes-only graph flagged the honest model on 100% of anchors.
   Resampling across use types crosses a real discontinuity (an office and a hospital have
   very different EUI). Restricting to same-use-type neighbours: FP 1.00 → 0.52.
2. **Graph must span the model's input space.** Buildings matching on attributes sit
   anywhere in the city, so omitted lat/lon variation reappeared as multimodality. Adding
   location as a coordinate helped, but then an isotropic 6-D metric fails differently:
   at every σ small enough to keep the honest model smooth, `median π_true = 0.000` —
   the probe never reaches the gate. By the time σ crosses 1960, honest FP is 0.28–0.40.
3. **The real cause — the DATA is multimodal in the probe metric.** Not discreteness (ESS
   45–395 in attribute space vs 13–572 geographically, so sampling is fine). Hartigan's dip
   on the raw covariates: `YearBuilt` p = 0.0000, `NumberofFloors` p = 0.0000 — construction
   booms and standard building heights. A neighbourhood therefore contains buildings from
   distinct clusters, and their responses inherit that clustering **under a perfectly smooth
   model with no gate at all.**

### The precondition this exposes

> On-manifold probing requires the **probe distribution itself to be locally smooth in the
> probe metric**. Otherwise the data's own clustering is read as model multimodality.

Ambient Gaussian probes satisfy this by construction — which is a real advantage that
partially offsets their off-manifold disadvantage, and which walks back the earlier
unqualified "make on-manifold primary." The correct statement is: on-manifold primary
**where the covariate distribution is locally smooth**, which is checkable (dip the
covariates).

Geography happened to satisfy it. That was luck, not principle.

### Partial fix

Residualizing the response on the probe displacement (removing the smooth part explained by
where the probe moved) before dipping:

| σ | raw honest FP | residualized honest FP |
|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 0.368 | 0.250 |
| 0.30 | 0.265 | **0.099** |
| 0.50 | 0.319 | **0.117** |

Confirms the mechanism; still 2× nominal, so not yet a valid test.

### Routes to the generalization claim

- **Discrete graphs** (electrical feeder, rate class, tariff schedule) — pool over a
  category rather than a continuous neighbourhood, sidestepping covariate smoothness
  entirely. Grid topology is the compelling energy case; data not currently available.
- **Proper residualization** — this is what v2's null generator was for, reappearing in a
  new place.
- **A larger or smoother dataset.**

### GLRT vs dip — empirical

In the contaminated attribute setting the GLRT flagged the honest model at 0.85 vs the
dip's 0.52. Exactly the predicted trade: the GLRT's narrower null ("a single Gaussian")
buys power but is far more exposed to non-Gaussian single-branch responses. **Dip stays
primary.** Since query reallocation already took the weak band from 0.472 to 0.875 with no
new assumptions, the GLRT's remaining value is small.

## 7c. Corrections and the current figure set

**Probe scope — state this on the poster.** All Seattle probes perturbed **location only**
(lat, lon); building attributes were held at the anchor's values. That is a
*feature-targeted* probe in the sense of v2 §2.2 — it assumes the gate is spatial. A
general auditor perturbing all inputs spreads variance across dimensions that do not touch
the boundary, and crossing probability depends only on variance along the boundary normal
(the σ_eff / α analysis in the June notes). The attribute-graph experiment was the attempt
to relax this and is where the covariate-clustering obstacle appeared.

**Spatial randomization, radius-matched.** The test statistic must match the method's reach.
Flags from the naive multi-scale scan sit further from boundaries precisely because the
method detects from further away, so a "within 100 m" statistic penalizes the extra reach:

| radius | observed | null mean | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 100 m | 0.233 | 0.174 | 0.175 |
| ≤ 400 m | 0.765 | 0.495 | 0.013 |
| **≤ 800 m** | **0.972** | 0.724 | **0.0025** |

800 m is taken from the independently measured power-vs-distance curve, not chosen for
significance. Report the full radius sweep so this is auditable.
